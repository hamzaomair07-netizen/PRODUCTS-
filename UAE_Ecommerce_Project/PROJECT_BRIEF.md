## 3. Definition of the opportunity

Find the intersection of:

1. A problem customers experience and will pay to solve.
2. Evidence of purchasing or purchase intent, with dates and geography.
3. A local offer gap that is commercially meaningful.
4. Demonstrable product performance that makes a short advertisement credible.
5. UAE supply available at a workable small-order cost.
6. A selling price customers accept after comparing alternatives.
7. Enough contribution to absorb realistic acquisition and fulfillment costs.

“New” alone is not enough. A product with no competitors may have no demand. Equally, ten sellers do not automatically prove saturation. Examine equivalent offers, prices, brands, ad repetition, fulfillment speed, and customer alternatives.

Distinguish:

- A new product.
- An established product that has recently accelerated abroad.
- A product newly gaining traction in the UAE.
- An old product with a genuinely underserved local use case.
- An old commodity given a new name or bundled without additional value.

Do not classify the last category as a discovery. The owner explicitly rejected that approach.

## 5. Brick: candidate dossier and limitations

### 5.1 What it is

The original **The Brick**, from **Brick LLC**, is a small physical NFC device used with a smartphone application. The user selects distracting apps, activates blocking, and normally returns to the physical device to unblock them. Its purpose is to introduce physical friction into habitual scrolling.

It is not a mobile network jammer, not a phone charger, and not a device that makes a smartphone completely unusable. Do not present it as a medical treatment or a guaranteed solution to addiction.

The company describes a magnetic body/base, no battery, no charging, and no Wi-Fi requirement for the physical device. This does not mean the companion application never needs internet access for download, account functions, or updates.

## 6. Evidence rules for the full study

Every material claim must be classified as one of:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|

| Platform indicator | Marketplace badge, review count, rank, or ad-library record |
| Third-party estimate | External methodology-dependent estimate |

| Unknown | Required evidence absent or inaccessible |

Record source URL, access date, measurement period, geography, exact SKU, currency, tax basis, and limitations. Capture important supplier quotes and offer screenshots with dates. Do not misrepresent a failed page load as verification.

For fresh trend analysis, prioritize the latest 30–90 days and compare consistent monthly or weekly observations. At project inception the focus is July–September 2026; September is incomplete on September 29. Compare completed periods or comparable partial periods, not a full month against an incomplete one.

Older information can establish product history or structural context but must not be labeled recent momentum. A “crawled today” label does not make the underlying measurement current.

Never fabricate:

- Keyword volumes, ad-library results, spend, CPC, CPM, conversion rates, or competitor profitability.
- Supplier quotes, local stock quantities, delivery guarantees, or warehouse locations.
- Market shares, number of unique competitors, or probability of success.
- Testimonials, before/after results, customer reviews, or purchase notifications.

When account access is unavailable, complete public-source work and specify the exact export needed. Do not pretend a public search replaces Keyword Planner, advertising account data, supplier confirmation, or real transactions.

## 9. Financial model: required inputs and definitions

Build an editable model with one authoritative set of assumptions and traceable calculations. Show low/base/high outcomes, but do not call them probabilities unless a defensible probability model supports them.

### 9.1 Include all relevant costs

- Unit purchase cost, tax treatment, and inbound local freight per unit.
- Samples, testing, supplier visits if necessary, and unsellable demo units.
- Packaging, labels, inserts, picking/packing, and owner's handling time.
- Outbound delivery, remote-area/weight surcharges, and courier minimum commitments.
- Failed delivery, second attempt, return-to-origin, return collection, inspection, and restocking.
- Damage, defects, warranty replacements, and inventory write-downs.
- Gateway percentage and fixed fees, COD fees, refund fees, disputes, and payout delay.
- Platform, domain, hosting, applications, email, and any incremental licensing/compliance costs.
- Creative production, AI subscriptions actually needed, sample filming, editing, and creator fees if proposed.
- Media spend and applicable nonrecoverable taxes on advertising services.
- Discounts, delivery subsidies, refunds, and promotional incentives.
- Inventory financing/cash tie-up and reserve requirements.

Show owner labor separately as both cash cost, when paid, and economic time cost. Do not claim a scalable margin that depends on unlimited unpaid work.

### 9.2 Tax and accounting basis

Distinguish customer cash collected from revenue net of remittable VAT. Confirm actual registration and input-tax recoverability. Do not automatically subtract output VAT for a nonregistered business or treat recoverable input VAT as permanent expense.

Use consistent tax treatment throughout. Track VAT cash timing separately when relevant. Unsold inventory is not automatically an expense; it is cash tied up, subject to impairment. Avoid counting an inventory purchase once as cash outflow and again as an additional expense beyond COGS in the same profit calculation.

### 9.3 Define the funnel and advertising metrics

Use a common period, campaign cohort, currency, and attribution basis:

```text
CPM = advertising spend / impressions × 1,000
Outbound CTR = outbound link clicks / impressions
Outbound CPC = advertising spend / outbound link clicks
Landing-page arrival rate = valid landing-page sessions / outbound clicks
Cost per landing-page session = advertising spend / valid landing-page sessions
Placed-order conversion = valid placed orders / valid landing-page sessions
Retained-order conversion = delivered, paid, nonrefunded orders / valid sessions
Placed-order CPA = advertising spend / valid placed orders
Retained-order acquisition cost = advertising spend / delivered, paid, nonrefunded orders
New-customer CAC = acquisition spend / new customers on the stated cost basis
```

Do not call cost per order “CAC” when repeat purchases or multiple orders per customer make them different. Explain media-only versus fully loaded acquisition costs. Separate prepaid orders from COD orders and orders placed from orders delivered and retained.

When using an impression-based funnel:

```text
Expected retained orders
  = impressions × outbound CTR × landing-page arrival rate
    × placed-order conversion × order-retention rate

Expected media cost per retained order
  = (CPM / 1,000)
    / (outbound CTR × arrival rate × placed-order conversion × retention rate)
```

Rates use decimals and matching denominators. If the conversion rate already means retained orders, do not multiply by retention again. Expected fractional orders in a forecast are not observed transactions.

### 9.4 Contribution and return measures

Define an expected cost per retained order using a reconciled cohort allocation. Include the costs of failed/refunded orders without recognizing their refunded revenue as retained revenue.

```text
Pre-ad contribution per retained order
  = net retained revenue per order
    − product cost consumed
    − fulfillment, payment, and packaging costs
    − allocated expected failure/return/warranty losses

Break-even media acquisition cost
  = pre-ad contribution per retained order

Target media acquisition cost
  = pre-ad contribution − desired contribution after advertising

Cohort profit
  = net retained revenue − all attributable variable costs
    − media spend − applicable fixed/setup costs

Net-revenue ROAS = net retained revenue / advertising spend
Gross platform ROAS = platform-attributed gross purchase value / advertising spend
Break-even net-revenue ROAS = net revenue per retained order / pre-ad contribution
Test ROI = test profit / explicitly defined total test costs
Capital return for period = period profit / explicitly defined capital base
ROE = period net income / average equity for the same period
```

Report the chosen ROI denominator; do not switch between ad spend, total costs, and capital invested. Do not annualize a seven-day result into a credible annual ROE forecast. ROE may not be meaningful before operations and equity balances exist; say so.

Model cash flow separately from profit: opening cash, inventory payments, ad payments, supplier/courier payments, payment settlements, reserves, and closing cash. Report the maximum funding gap and the ability to restock before gateway/COD remittances arrive.

## 10. What “more than 75% success” must mean

The owner's desired threshold must be treated seriously as a risk requirement. It must not become an invented confidence score.

First define the success event, for example:

> “For a specified test cohort and budget, delivered and retained orders produce positive contribution after advertising; delivery commitments are met; after allowing for returns, the next small reorder is financeable.”

Also report separately whether the full test recovers samples, creative, and setup expenses. Positive unit contribution is not the same as a profitable launch.

Do not confuse:

- 75% probability of business success.
- 75% advertising conversion.
- 75% of tested creatives performing well.
- A 75/100 qualitative product score.
- A confidence interval or posterior probability under a statistical model.

No public-source research in this project has established a 75% success probability. Do not claim that it has.

Required approach:

1. State the event, time horizon, spend ceiling, and acceptable loss before the test.
2. Identify uncertainty in supplier cost, click/session cost, conversion, retention, and delivery cost.
3. Use scenarios initially; label them assumptions, not probabilities.
4. Once observations exist, report sample sizes and uncertainty intervals, accounting for uncompleted delivery/return outcomes.
5. If estimating a probability, disclose the model, prior/base rates, data, dependence assumptions, and sensitivity. A conversion-rate posterior alone is not a probability of commercial success.
6. If evidence cannot support the requested 75%, state “not established” and identify the smallest practical evidence-gathering step.

Do not select a sample size merely to obtain a desired answer. Twenty retained orders may be an operational checkpoint but do not automatically establish statistical confidence. A seven-day test may be inconclusive, especially when acquisition costs are high and sample counts are low.

## 15. Seven-day advertising experiment

### 15.1 Day 0: readiness comes first

The seven days mean a paid test **after** readiness. They are not a promise to launch seven days from today regardless of unresolved issues.

Before paid launch:

- Exact product, sample quality, UAE stock, and supplier terms verified.
- Inventory reserved or physically held, including a replacement allowance where affordable.
- Courier test and realistic supported delivery zones/cutoffs established.
- Actual unit economics and test loss ceiling approved.
- Landing page, mobile checkout, payment confirmation, tracking, and customer messages tested.
- Events deduplicated; UTMs and order IDs connect advertising to actual sales.
- Required commercial/consumer information and support process ready.

### 15.2 Illustrative staged media schedule

This schedule uses the provisional AED 600 ceiling. Adapt it to economics and platform practicality. Never spend simply because a day has arrived.

| Day | Maximum incremental media, AED | Main question |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 60 | Does the correct audience reach a functioning page and checkout? |
| 2 | 60 | Are there qualified sessions and meaningful shopping actions? |
| 3 | 80 | Which message attracts buyers rather than just viewers? |
| 4 | 80 | Can the most credible offer produce valid paid/confirmed orders? |
| 5 | 100 | Do early orders deliver successfully and economics remain plausible? |
| 6 | 100 | Is performance repeatable within the remaining stock and budget? |
| 7 | 120 | Does the cohort justify continuation, revision, or stopping? |
| Total ceiling | 600 | Unspent allocations remain unspent if a gate fails |

Start with a small number of distinct creatives. Do not make changes so frequently that no comparison is interpretable. Set evaluation windows and minimum observation requirements before launch, calibrated to the product and budget.

At AED 30 per retained order, AED 600 corresponds arithmetically to 20 retained orders; at AED 60, it corresponds to 10. These are sensitivity illustrations, not promised outcomes. Check inventory capacity before planning those order counts.

### 15.3 Stop, revise, continue, and scale rules

Provide numeric thresholds in the final study **after** calculating the actual contribution and plausible funnel. Do not invent universal CTR/CPC benchmarks as pass criteria.

- **Immediate stop:** invalid tracking, broken checkout, inaccurate product claims, missing stock, inability to fulfill, or materially defective sample/product.
- **Spending pause:** cumulative exposure reaches the pre-agreed learning/loss ceiling, or actual economics no longer support the allowable acquisition cost.
- **Revise creative:** evidence suggests attention/qualification mismatch; change the message deliberately, not all variables together.
- **Revise page/offer:** qualified traffic reaches the page but drops at a specific step; inspect price, compatibility, trust, delivery, and checkout friction.
- **Continue cautiously:** promising retained-order contribution with unresolved uncertainty and sufficient budget/stock; identify the additional evidence required.
- **Inconclusive:** insufficient valid observations or incomplete delivery/return outcomes. Do not relabel this a success or failure to force a decision.
- **Scale:** positive contribution survives conservative return assumptions, delivery promises are met, replenishment is verified, and cash can fund the next batch. Increase in controlled increments with a fresh loss limit.

Do not decide commercial success on ad-manager purchases alone. Day 7 is a preliminary review; reconcile final retained orders once relevant delivery/return outcomes mature.

- **Hold for evidence:** could be viable but a decisive input is missing.
